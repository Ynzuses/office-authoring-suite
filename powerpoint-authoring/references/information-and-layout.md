# Information and layout

Read for a new layout or feedback about crowding, fragmented text, uneven whitespace or weak emphasis. This is a decision guide, not a required theme, picture quota or word-count limit.

## Decide what belongs on screen

Establish the slide's principal purpose in the talk. Several comparisons or linked steps can serve one purpose; title, transition and reference slides have different jobs. Keep a supplied deck's required chapter headings where they matter, and express the message in a subtitle, visual or concise takeaway when renaming headings would disrupt the template.

Separate three roles before resizing anything:

| Role | Placement decision |
| --- | --- |
| Core message and evidence | Keep the claim, causal steps or comparison the audience must follow, with the relevant figure or data |
| Qualifications that change interpretation | Keep relevant population, denominator, period, units and evidence limitations adjacent to the claim; do not hide them in tiny credits |
| Supporting explanation | When revision is authorized, move derivations, secondary examples, repeated prose and unused historical detail to notes or an explicitly permitted appendix |

Preserve earlier notes and meaningful source wording in the task's revision history. Reorganize the new speaking guidance so the presenter can expand the displayed anchors naturally. Do not turn notes into an unrelated lecture or assume every retained background paragraph must be spoken. A full script or extra handout remains a separate deliverable unless requested.

For conservative editing, first remove duplication and shorten wording without changing the claim. Record substantive corrections separately with their evidence. A crowded team-authored slide does not grant permission to rewrite the scientific argument freely.

## Choose the relationship before the container

| Content relationship | Useful organization | Typical repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two alternatives or types | Matched columns or a row-aligned comparison | Compare the same dimensions; put explanatory detail in notes instead of long asymmetric paragraphs |
| Mechanism or ordered response | Connected stages with actors and actions | Keep the causal middle steps; use an anatomy/experimental image where it provides context |
| A few independent statistics | Clearly labelled, aligned metric regions | Put each number next to its measure, population and period; a trend chart is unnecessary if the talk only needs current scale |
| Trend, association or distribution | A readable chart that supports that specific message | Select relevant series, show honest axes/uncertainty, and explain the comparison; do not suppress uncertainty when the claim depends on it |
| A clinical or experimental appearance | A large figure with a concise interpretive caption | Reserve useful image area first; retain the population and what the image can and cannot demonstrate |
| Several symptom/topic anchors | Consistent rows or meaningful groups | Keep complete phrases and one concise explanation per anchor where appropriate, rather than scattering clauses into separate badges |

Do not directly rank estimates with different populations, definitions or methods on a shared comparison axis. Separate them and expose the qualifications. Removing a secondary series or confidence interval from a scale-only display can be appropriate if it does not strengthen the claim, remains traceable in supporting records, and the resulting statement retains its evidential limits.

Decorative icons orient or mark actions; they cannot replace a relevant scientific figure. A large graphic is useful only when it explains the displayed content. Avoid collecting images merely to fill empty space.

## Repair balance and fragmentation

Establish a common alignment grid for headings, figures, text and citations. In parallel columns, align corresponding levels, not just outside rectangles. Put labels and icons close to what they identify. Use one clear reading order instead of several competing emphasis zones.

Whitespace should separate related groups and give the visual room to breathe. Judge the distribution of visual weight at fit-to-slide: a large empty strip next to a dense text block may call for changing their proportions, shortening that block or enlarging a useful figure. It does not automatically call for another icon, paragraph or card.

Avoid fixed-height cards that leave a short item floating while another overflows. Adjust the content or layout to the actual relationship. Consistent spacing does not require filling every region equally or making every page use the same card pattern. Preserve the deck's fonts, colors, branding and section rhythm while varying the body layout when content needs it.

Choose line breaks at phrase boundaries. Group a heading with its explanation; do not break a coherent sentence into a collection of isolated labels solely to make it look designed. Use arrows for a real sequence or causal link, not as decoration. Suppress unnecessary shadows and redundant title/icon ornament.

## Learn from examples and verify the repair

When asked to learn from good presentations, inspect actual slides or before/after examples rather than just searching for lists of tips. Record the source and the specific technique applied. Study hierarchy, figure size, grouping and information selection; do not copy another institution's identity or disregard the supplied template.

Useful primary teaching references include [MIT Slide Design](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/aeroastro/commkit/slide-design/) and [MIT Slide Presentation](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/eecs/commkit/slideshow/). They are optional design references, not permission to import their copyrighted slide artwork. Recheck any material used for a task and record its actual reuse terms.

Preview the changed data/text page and an image page before completing the deck. At an ordinary fitted size, check whether the audience can identify the message and follow the reading order without reading the whole script. Inspect detail size separately for clipping, source text and image boundaries. Meaningful hierarchy, balance and recognisability need visual judgement; word counts and geometry cannot certify them.

Apply feedback to analogous pages within the authorized section. Record what stayed visible and what moved to speaking notes, then compare numbers, qualifications and causal relationships with the source. Keep both the content decision and final visual evidence traceable. See [design and validation](design-and-validation.md) for file-bound checks.
